# NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0 --- Important Points

**Source:** NIST CSWP 29, February 26, 2024\
**Section:** Preface

## 1. Purpose of the CSF 2.0

-   The Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0 helps organizations **manage
    and reduce cybersecurity risks**.
-   It is designed for organizations of all sizes and sectors,
    including:
    -   Industry
    -   Government and other organizations
    -   Academia
    -   Non-profit organizations
-   It can be useful regardless of an organization's cybersecurity
    maturity or technical sophistication.

## 2. No One-Size-Fits-All Approach

-   The CSF does **not** prescribe one fixed approach for every
    organization.
-   Organizations differ in:
    -   Common and unique risks
    -   Risk appetite and risk tolerance
    -   Missions and objectives
    -   Technologies and operating environments
-   Therefore, each organization should adapt and implement the CSF
    according to its own needs.

## 3. Cybersecurity as Part of Enterprise Risk Management

The CSF is intended to help organizations consider cybersecurity
alongside other enterprise risks, such as: - Financial risks - Privacy
risks - Supply-chain risks - Reputational risks - Technological risks -
Physical risks

## 4. Desired Outcomes

-   The CSF describes **desired cybersecurity outcomes** in language
    intended for a broad audience.
-   It can be understood by executives, managers, and cybersecurity
    practitioners, including people with different levels of technical
    expertise.
-   Its outcomes are **sector-, country-, and technology-neutral**.
-   This flexibility helps organizations address their own risks,
    technologies, and missions.
-   The outcomes are mapped directly to potential security controls that
    organizations can consider to mitigate cybersecurity risks.

## 5. The CSF Is Not Prescriptive

-   The CSF helps users understand and select cybersecurity outcomes.
-   It does not require one specific method for achieving those
    outcomes.
-   Organizations choose the approaches and controls that fit their
    circumstances.

## 6. Supporting Resources and Tools

Resources that complement the CSF include: - **Quick Start Guides
(QSGs):** Help users begin using the CSF and explore it further. -
**Online resources:** Offer additional guidance on achieving outcomes. -
**Downloadable formats and tools:** Help organizations automate parts of
their processes. - **Implementation Examples:** Provide examples of how
outcomes may be achieved. - **Informative References:** Connect CSF
outcomes with relevant guidance and resources.

The CSF and its supporting resources can be viewed together as a **"CSF
portfolio."**

## 7. New Emphasis in CSF 2.0

Compared with previous versions, CSF 2.0 highlights: - The importance of
**governance**. - The importance of **supply chains**. - Making Quick
Start Guides useful and accessible to smaller organizations as well as
larger ones. - Providing Implementation Examples and Informative
References, which are available online and updated regularly.

## 8. Organizational Profiles

-   Organizations can create **current-state** and **target-state
    Organizational Profiles**.
-   These profiles help compare:
    -   Where the organization is now.
    -   Where it wants or needs to be.
-   This comparison can help organizations implement and assess security
    controls more quickly.

## 9. Continuous Cybersecurity Risk Management

-   Cybersecurity risks continue to expand and change.
-   Managing these risks must be an **ongoing, continuous process**.
-   The CSF is intended to be useful for:
    -   Organizations just beginning to address cybersecurity.
    -   Organizations with mature cybersecurity programs and
        well-resourced teams.
-   It is designed to provide useful guidance over the long term.

## Key Terms to Remember

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Term                                Meaning in this section
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  **Cybersecurity risk**              Risk an organization manages and
                                      seeks to reduce through
                                      cybersecurity practices.

  **Risk appetite and tolerance**     The level and types of risk an
                                      organization is willing or able to
                                      accept.

  **Desired outcomes**                The cybersecurity results an
                                      organization aims to achieve.

  **Non-prescriptive**                Provides guidance on outcomes
                                      without requiring one specific
                                      implementation method.

  **Quick Start Guides (QSGs)**       Supporting guides that help
                                      organizations begin using the CSF.

  **Governance**                      An important area emphasized in CSF
                                      2.0.

  **Supply chain**                    An important risk area emphasized
                                      in CSF 2.0.

  **Organizational Profile**          A way to describe an organization's
                                      current or target cybersecurity
                                      posture.

  **CSF portfolio**                   The CSF together with its
                                      supplementary resources and tools.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

## Quick Revision --- Most Important Points

1.  CSF 2.0 helps organizations of all sizes and sectors manage and
    reduce cybersecurity risks.
2.  It is flexible; there is no one-size-fits-all implementation.
3.  Cybersecurity should be considered alongside financial, privacy,
    supply-chain, reputational, technological, and physical risks.
4.  The framework describes desired outcomes and is not prescriptive
    about exactly how to achieve them.
5.  Quick Start Guides, tools, Implementation Examples, and Informative
    References support its use.
6.  CSF 2.0 gives added attention to governance and supply chains.
7.  Current-state and target-state Organizational Profiles help
    organizations identify the gap between their present and desired
    cybersecurity posture.
8.  Cybersecurity risk management is continuous.
