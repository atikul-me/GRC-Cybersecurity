# The NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0 — Overview & Study Notes

* **Document Reference:** NIST CSWP 29
* **Release Date:** February 26, 2024
* **Focus Area:** Cybersecurity Governance, Risk Management, and Framework Architecture

---

## 📌 1. Introduction & Purpose

Version 2.0 of the **NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)** provides a foundational, flexible taxonomy of high-level cybersecurity outcomes to help organizations of all sizes and sectors manage cybersecurity risks. 

* **Outcome-Driven (Not Prescriptive):** The CSF describes *what* desirable cybersecurity outcomes an organization can aspire to achieve, rather than prescribing *how* they must be achieved.
* **Universal Applicability:** Designed to be **sector-, country-, and technology-neutral**, allowing adoption across industry, government, academia, and nonprofit organizations regardless of program maturity.

---

## 🏗️ 2. Core Components of NIST CSF 2.0

The framework consists of three primary interconnected components:

### 1. CSF Core
The **nucleus** of the CSF—a structured hierarchy of high-level cybersecurity outcomes designed to be understood by a broad audience (executives, managers, and technical practitioners alike). It is organized into:
* **Functions**
* **Categories**
* **Subcategories**

### 2. CSF Organizational Profiles
A mechanism for describing an organization's **current** and/or **target** cybersecurity posture in terms of the CSF Core's outcomes, helping align security efforts with mission objectives and risk tolerances.

### 3. CSF Tiers
Applied to Organizational Profiles to characterize the **rigor** of an organization's cybersecurity risk governance and management practices. Tiers provide context on how an organization views cybersecurity risks and the processes in place to manage them.

---

## 🛠️ 3. Supplementary Online Resources

To help organizations understand, adopt, and implement the framework, NIST provides a suite of complementary online resources:

* **Informative References:** Mappings pointing to existing global standards, guidelines, frameworks, regulations, and policies for each outcome.
* **Implementation Examples:** Practical illustrations of potential ways to achieve specific CSF outcomes.
* **Quick-Start Guides:** Actionable guidance for adopting CSF 2.0 and transitioning from previous framework versions.
* **Community Profiles & Organizational Profile Templates:** Reusable baselines to help organizations put the CSF into practice and prioritize risk management efforts.

---

## 🎯 4. How Organizations Use the CSF

By combining the **CSF Core, Profiles, and Tiers** with supplementary resources, organizations can systematically:

1. **Understand and Assess:** Describe their current or target cybersecurity posture, identify security gaps, and track progress toward remediation.
2. **Prioritize:** Identify, organize, and prioritize risk management actions aligned with organizational mission, legal/regulatory requirements, and governance expectations.
3. **Communicate:** Establish a common language to discuss cybersecurity risks, capabilities, needs, and expectations both internally and externally.
